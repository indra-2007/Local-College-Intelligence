                                   Unit - I                                           9   Hours
 Vectors in 2D and 3D: Vectors in 2D and 3D, Linear Independence, Length of a vector, Dot
 Product and Orthogonal Projections, Linear maps in 2D and 3D, eigenvalue and eigenvectors.
                                   Unit - II                                          9   Hours
 Vector Spaces-I: Fields, vector spaces, vector subspaces, basis, dimension of a vector subspace
 Matrices and the four fundamental subspaces associated with them, rank-nullity theorem.
                                  Unit - III                                          9   Hours
 Vector Spaces-II: Linear maps, Inner product, vector norm, orthogonality, orthogonal projections,
 Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization, QR decomposition. Approximating vectors in other subspaces.
                                  Unit - IV                                           9   Hours
 Matrix Decompositions and Least Squares: Least square solutions. Real symmetric matrices
 and properties, Diagonalization. Quadratic forms. SVD and pseudoinverse.
                                   Unit - V                                           9   Hours
 Linear Algebra Applications: In Probability and Statistics, Convolution, Linear Programming.
  Course Outcomes: After completing the course, the students will be able to:
 CO1: Apply vector operations, including dot and cross products, to calculate geometric properties
   like area, volume, and orthogonal projections in 2D and 3D systems.
 CO2: Analyze the properties of vector spaces and subspaces to determine basis, dimension, and the
   relationships between the four fundamental subspaces of a matrix.
 CO3: Execute matrix factorization and orthogonalization techniques, such as Gram-Schmidt (GSO)
   and QR decomposition, to simplify complex linear systems.
 CO4: Evaluate linear systems using eigenvalues, eigenvectors, and Least Squares solutions to approx-
   imate data and solve optimization problems involving pseudoinverses.
 CO5: Design and implement linear algebraic solutions to real-world problems in statistics, signal
   processing via convolution, and linear programming, critically connecting theoretical tools to
   machine learning workflows and data science pipelines.
  References

       Least Squares. Cambridge University Press, 2018
   [5] H. Anton and A. Kaul, Elementary Linear Algebra, 12th. Wiley, 2019
