5.1 BE (HONORS)
An increasingly interconnected global economy as a result of globalization is creating
collaborative opportunities and competitive pressures requiring broadly educated engineers and
business professionals. Also, the next generation engineers have to practice an interdisciplinary
approach to problem solving.

To encourage and groom engineering students during their engineering program to become self-
motivated independent investigators, critical thinkers and problem solvers, good communicators,
effective managers and lifelong learners, AICTE has introduced a scheme wherein a student has
to earn 18 or more additional credits through Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs).

To cater the engineering skill requirements of the next generation due to continuously changing
expectations of the industry, society and global stakeholder community owing to technology
growth and globalization, RVCE intends to equip its students with interdisciplinary knowledge.
By introducing this scheme, RVCE is one of the first few technical institutions to implement a few
objectives of NEP 2020 with immediate effect.

The objectives of decorating the students with superior academic ability with Honours are to:
a. Encourage the advanced levels of study and multidisciplinary learning
b. Specialize in a focused area of your interest
c. Learn new skills and methodologies pertaining to your areas of interest
d. Cultivate a sustaining interest in advanced education and research
e. Gain recognition at the time of graduation for advanced, distinguished work as an
     undergraduate

5.2 BE (MINOR)
A minor is a secondary field of study that undergraduate students can pursue in addition to their
major. While the major is the primary focus of a student's acadernic program and is usually chosen
based on career goals and interests, a minor provides an opportunity to explore another area of
interest in a more limited fashion. To enhance employability skills and impart deep knowledge in
emerging areas that are usually not covered in the Undergraduate Degree credit framework,
AICTE has come up with the concept of a 'Minor Degree' in emerging areas. A minor Degree
shall carry 18 credits in addition to the credits essential for obtaining the Under Graduate Degree
in Major Discipline (i.e. 160 credits for regular students and 120 credits for lateral entry students).

Objectives:
a. The key objectives of offering B.E., with Minor programs are:
b. To enable students to pursue an allied academic interest in contemporary areas
c. To expand the domain knowledge of the students in one of the other branches of engineering.
d. To provide an academic mechanism for fulfilling the multidisciplinary demands of industries.
e. To increase the employability of undergraduate students, keeping in view better opportunities
     in interdisciplinary areas of engineering & technology.
f.   To offer knowledge in the areas that are identified as emerging technologies/thrust areas of
     Engineering.

f. A student cannot earn the Minor after he/she has already earned a bachelor's degree by earning

component of engineering learning. Through the Continuing Education and Skill Development
(CESD) initiative, the Institute has established multidisciplinary Skill Laboratories that bridge the
gap between theoretical concepts and practical engineering applications.

The Skill Lab framework is designed to provide students with structured, hands-on learning
experiences using modern equipment, industrial software, advanced laboratory facilities, and real-
world engineering practices. These programmes foster technical competence, problem-solving
ability, creativity, innovation, teamwork, and professional ethics while preparing students for
emerging technologies and future industrial challenges.

The Skill Labs span a wide range of engineering disciplines and are aligned with the evolving
requirements of industry. The programmes include:

The skill development framework follows a progressive three-level approach that ensures
continuous competency development throughout the undergraduate programme:

a.   Basic Engineering Skills (Semester I & II): Development of foundational engineering skills,
     laboratory practices, measurements, fabrication, computing, and engineering fundamentals.

b.   Domain Skills (Semester III & IV): Discipline-specific practical training using advanced
     software tools, laboratory equipment, simulations, and industrial methodologies.

c.   Industrial Skills (Semester V & VI): Advanced industry-oriented technologies, automation,
     artificial intelligence, digital manufacturing, product development, Industry 4.0, electric
     mobility, robotics, cloud technologies, and interdisciplinary projects.

Through these multidisciplinary Skill Labs and industry-integrated training programmes, RVCE
reinforces its commitment to producing competent, innovative, and industry-ready engineering
graduates equipped with the knowledge, technical expertise, and professional skills required to
address global engineering challenges and contribute to sustainable technological development.
