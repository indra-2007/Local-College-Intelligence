                                  Unit - I                                           9   Hours
 Introduction to Quantum Computing: Basics of quantum mechanics for computing, Qubits
 vs classical bits, Quantum entanglement, Superposition and measurement, Bloch sphere represen-
 tation.Quantum Building Blocks: Quantum mechanics of Photon Polarization, Single Quantum
 bits, Single Qubit Measurement, A Quantum key Distribution Protocol, State Space of a Single-Qubit
 System.
                                  Unit - II                                          9   Hours
 Multiple-Qubit Systems: Quantum State Spaces, Direct Sums of Vector Spaces, Tensor Products
 of Vector Spaces, State Space of an n-Qubit System, Entangled States, MultiQubit Measurement,
 QKD using Entangled states.
                                 Unit - III                                          9   Hours
 Measurement of Multiple-Qubit States: Dirac Bra/Ket Notation for Linear Transformation,
 Projection Operators for Measurement, Hermitian Operator Formalism for Measurement, EPR Para-
 dox and Bells Theorem.
                                 Unit - IV                                           9   Hours
 Quantum State Transformations: Unitary transformations, No-Cloning Principle, Some Simple
 Quantum Gates, Pauli transformations, Hadamard Transformations, Multiple-Qubit Transforma-
 tions, ControlledNOT and other singly controlled gates, Applications of Simple Gates, Dense coding,
 Quantum teleportation
                                  Unit - V                                           9   Hours
 Quantum Versions of Classical Computations: Reversible Classical Computations to Quantum
 Computations, Reversible and Quantum Versions of Simple Classical Gates, Reversible Implemen-
 tations of Classical Circuits, Example Programs for Arithmetic Operations.Quantum Computing
 Platforms and Applications: NISQ (Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum) Era of Quantum Com-
 puting and limitations, Quantum simulators and tools, Applications in optimization, cryptography
 and AI, Quantum Hardware – Goals and Challenges.
  Course Outcomes: After completing the course, the students will be able to:
 CO1: Analyze the principles of quantum mechanics, qubits, superposition, entanglement, Bloch
   sphere representation, and quantum state spaces to distinguish quantum computation from clas-
   sical computation.
 CO2: Examine single-qubit and multi-qubit quantum systems by analyzing quantum state represen-
   tations, tensor products, quantum measurements, and quantum key distribution protocols for
   secure quantum communication.
 CO3: Evaluate quantum state measurement techniques, Hermitian operators, projection measure-
   ments, Bells theorem, and EPR paradox to interpret the behavior and correlation of quantum
   systems.
 CO4: Design quantum circuits by applying unitary transformations, quantum gates, controlled oper-
   ations, dense coding, and quantum teleportation to solve computational problem
